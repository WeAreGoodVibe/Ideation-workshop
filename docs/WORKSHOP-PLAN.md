# The 90 minutes, and what the board is for

Watches of Switzerland. Eight people from Finance and Purchasing. One goal: a long list of places Claude gives them hours back, each one described as a thing that can be built.

## The one reframe before anything else

**Ideation is validating, not learning.** You have no discovery session behind you, so this workshop has to do both jobs in 90 minutes. That is why the run sheet spends 40 of the 90 minutes walking the process. Ideas that come out of a process walk are located and sized. Ideas that come out of "what could AI do for you?" are opinions.

Do not ask the room for AI ideas. Ask them how the work happens. The board does the translation.

## The run sheet

| Block | Minutes | What happens | Board view |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Frame | 5 | North star, scope test, how the board works. "You will see the count grow." | Run sheet |
| 2. Systems | 10 | Confirm or correct the assumed stack. Where does data get re-keyed. | Systems |
| 3. Finance walk | 15 | Phase by phase. Five facts per phase. AI listens. | Process walk |
| 4. Purchasing walk | 15 | Same. Finance listens for shared pain. | Process walk |
| 5. Pause | 5 | Stretch. Facilitator tidies titles, merges duplicates, tags functions. | Opportunities |
| 6. Validate and vote | 20 | Is the pain real, is the direction right. Three dots each. Watch for convergence. | Opportunities |
| 7. Second viewpoint | 10 | The reveal. Consultant blind spots plus what Claude wrote from the transcript. | Second viewpoint |
| 8. Commit | 10 | Top five, owners, first build this week. Export. | Opportunities |

## The questions that matter most

The engagement field guide has the full bank. Four questions carry the day because each one maps a sentence to a Claude surface:

| When they say | It is a | Build |
|---|---|---|
| "Every Monday", "every month-end", "when it arrives" | Scheduled Task | A prompt on a timer with the connectors it needs |
| "I always paste in the same policy / template / export" | Project | Standing context: instructions plus reference files |
| "There is a procedure, it just lives in my head" | Skill | The SOP written as instructions Claude loads on demand |
| "Go through the folder and produce the pack" | Cowork | A task across files and apps on their machine |
| "Claude cannot see that system" | Connector setup | Connect NetSuite, SharePoint, Outlook or Teams first |

Ask these three in every phase:

1. What do you produce that looks the same each time?
2. What arrives that you wish was already summarised?
3. What would you hand to a competent new starter with a written procedure?

## What you were not thinking about

**1. The transcript is not the product. The tagged register is.** A raw transcript from Wispr Flow is an input. The output the room needs to see is: title, function, phase, surface, build type, pain, what Claude does. The board forces every idea through that shape.

**2. Function tags are not enough. Location is.** "Finance or Purchasing or both" tells you who owns it. The process phase tells you where it sits and lets the heat map appear. When accounts payable lights up with six ideas, that is one foundations problem, not six features.

**3. A count is not a metric.** Forty ideas is not better than twelve. The room can rank six clusters. Cap the clusters at about six on the day, and use the count badge for momentum, not for scoring.

**4. The second viewpoint needs a "why".** A list of clever ideas from outside reads as showing off. The same list with "why you did not raise it" against each one reads as insight. The board makes that field mandatory.

**5. Value and Ease are scored by different people.** The room owns Value. You own Ease. Do not score either in this session. Leave the columns blank in the export and run prioritisation as a separate room.

**6. What will not go through Claude.** Customer names, serial numbers, salaries. Ask it in block 2, write it down, and say it out loud when the AI starts listening. It is the difference between a room that talks and a room that watches its words.

**7. The failed tool.** Every finance team has a workflow tool nobody used. Ask what it was and why. The answer is your anti-requirement list and the accuracy bar an automation must clear.

**8. The room's own devices.** If the board is published as an Artifact with a shared store, all eight can open it on their laptop and vote from their seat. That changes the energy of block 6.

## Devil's advocate

**"Listening AI during a workshop is a gimmick."** It can be. It is not a gimmick if the room sees an idea land with a number thirty seconds after someone described their Monday morning. That moment is the demonstration of what you are selling. If the transcript source is flaky, the demonstration fails in front of the client. So test the source on the day, and have dictation as the fallback: press the Wispr hotkey and summarise what was just said in one sentence. That is enough for the extractor.

**"Eight people is too many for 90 minutes."** It is too many for one conversation. Split blocks 3 and 4: Finance talks while Purchasing listens for shared pain, then swap. Do not let the two groups talk at once.

**"They will ask for a chatbot."** They will. Park it with the reason: chat is the surface everyone already has; the value is in the scheduled tasks, projects and skills that remove the recurring work. Point at the board.

## What comes out

- An Excel workbook with five tabs: the register, the new ideas, the systems, the lifecycle map with counts, and a read me.
- A shortlist of five with owners.
- The first skill to write together, named in the room.

The prioritisation session is a different room on a different day. Bring the workbook with Ease pre-scored.
